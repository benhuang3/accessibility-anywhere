Puppy and kitty are best friends

A golden puppy named Biscuit and a gray kitten named Pepper were found within a week of each other. Now they cannot be separated.

By Nora Whitfield · Staff Writer · Updated 11:42 AM · 6 min read · 214 comments

ShareTweetEmailPrintSaveComment

When the Hendersons brought home a golden puppy named Biscuit last spring, they expected a lot of chewed slippers and a few sleepless nights. They did not expect a gray kitten to climb through the garden fence a week later and decide that the new puppy was exactly the friend she had been looking for.

The kitten, who the family named Pepper, had been wandering the neighborhood alone for several days. Neighbors say she was hungry and tired. She made her way across three yards before curling up next to Biscuit's bed on the back porch. Biscuit, who is only four months old, did not bark or run. He simply sighed and rested his chin on her back.

"We stood in the kitchen with our coffee, watching through the window, and nobody said a word," said Ellen Henderson. "We just knew she was staying."

Since then the pair have been inseparable. They share a water bowl, take afternoon naps in the same patch of sunlight, and play a game that appears to involve Pepper hiding behind the laundry basket while Biscuit pretends he cannot see her. When the veterinarian came for their first checkups, Biscuit insisted on sitting in the room for Pepper's turn, and Pepper returned the favor.

Animal behavior experts say friendships between dogs and cats are more common than people think, especially when the animals meet when they are young. "Puppies and kittens are both curious and playful, and they learn each other's signals quickly," said Dr. Anita Rao, a veterinary behaviorist. "A wagging tail and a purr may mean different things, but over time they figure out the translation."

The Hendersons say the friendship has changed the household. Their teenage son, who used to stay in his room after school, now rushes home to take both animals for a walk, Pepper riding in a canvas bag with her head poking out. Their grandmother, who lives next door, has taken to stopping by with treats and a folding chair so she can sit in the yard and watch the two play.

Not every day is calm. Biscuit has learned that Pepper is faster than he is, and she has learned that Biscuit's tail is an excellent toy. But when the day is over, the family says, the two curl up together on the same blanket by the back door, the puppy's paw draped over the kitten, and the house gets quiet.

"People ask us if we are going to find her a different home," Ellen said, laughing. "Where would she go? She is already home. And so is he."
